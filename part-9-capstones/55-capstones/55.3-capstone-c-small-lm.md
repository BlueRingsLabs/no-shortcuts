# 55.3 Capstone C: train and post-train a small language model

*About 60 hours, plus compute. Prerequisites: Part IV and Part V. Code to start from: `part-5-language-models/_shared/`.*

Nobody will pay you to pretrain a frontier model from this course. People will pay you to understand one well enough to fine-tune it,
evaluate it, serve it and know when it's lying, and there's no faster way to get that understanding than doing every stage yourself at a
scale that fits on one machine. You'll build a small language model end to end: data, tokenizer, pretraining, a scaling experiment,
post-training, and an evaluation you'd defend in front of skeptics. The model will be bad. That's fine. The engineering and the honesty
of the evaluation are what's graded.

## Budget

Pick one and state it in your report: **CPU only** (models of 1 to 10M parameters, a few hours of training), or **one consumer GPU** or a
rented one for a few dollars (10 to 100M parameters). Everything below scales to either. Log the compute you actually used, in FLOPs (35.4)
and in hours.

## What to build

**1. A pretraining corpus** (35.3), 20M to 500M tokens, from sources whose license allows it: public-domain books (Project Gutenberg),
Wikipedia dumps (CC BY-SA), permissively licensed code, government documents, or this course. Write the pipeline: extraction, language
filtering, quality heuristics, exact and near-duplicate removal (MinHash), PII scrubbing, and a held-out split made **before** any
deduplication decision that could leak across it. Document what you removed and why, with counts.

**2. A tokenizer** (35.1): train BPE on your corpus. Compare vocabulary sizes on compression (bytes per token) and on the model's final
loss per *byte* (not per token: losses with different tokenizers aren't comparable otherwise).

**3. Pretraining** (35.2, 35.5, 26.5): a decoder-only transformer with the modern choices (RoPE, RMSNorm, SwiGLU; justify any you drop),
mixed precision if you have a GPU, a warmup and cosine schedule, gradient clipping, checkpointing and resumption (test it: kill the job
and resume, and show the loss curve is continuous), and tracking (48.1).

**4. A scaling experiment** (35.4): at least four model sizes, each trained for a compute-appropriate number of tokens, and your own
fitted scaling curve of loss against compute. Predict the loss of a fifth, larger model from the curve before training it, then train it
and report the error of your prediction.

**5. Supervised fine-tuning** (38.1): an instruction dataset of at least 2,000 examples that you build (templated from structured data,
written by you, or generated by a larger model whose license allows it, clearly stated). Loss masking on the prompt, a chat template, and
a held-out set of *phrasings* as well as of questions (38.1 shows why).

**6. One of** (a) **LoRA** fine-tuning of the same model compared with full fine-tuning, on quality, forgetting and memory (38.2); or
(b) **preference optimization** with DPO on pairs you construct, measuring what it changes and what it breaks (38.3); or (c) **RL with a
verifiable reward** on a task you can check in code (arithmetic, unit conversions, small puzzles), with GRPO (38.4).

**7. Evaluation** (39.1), the part that decides the grade:
- perplexity per byte on held-out text from your corpus and from a different domain;
- task accuracy on the post-training task with confidence intervals and several prompt variants;
- a contamination check between your evaluation sets and your training data (n-gram overlap), with what you did about hits;
- a comparison with a trivial baseline (a unigram or n-gram model, 34.1) so the numbers mean something;
- a qualitative error analysis of 50 outputs, categorized.

**8. Serving** (37.1, 37.2): a generation loop with a KV cache, temperature and top-p sampling, streaming output, and measured tokens per
second at batch 1 and batch 8. Quantize the weights to int8 (37.3) and report the quality and speed change.

## Deliverables

- The repository, reproducible from raw data to the evaluated model with one documented command per stage.
- A **technical report** (8 to 15 pages) in the style of a model release paper: data, architecture, training, scaling results with your
  prediction's error, post-training, evaluation with intervals, limitations. Every number traceable to a tracked run.
- A **model card** (54.3) and a **datasheet** for your corpus.
- The loss curves, the scaling plot, and a table of every run you did, including the failed ones and why they failed.

## How it will be judged

| criterion | weight |
|---|---|
| evaluation honesty: held-out design, contamination check, intervals, baselines, error analysis | 30% |
| data pipeline: licensing, filtering, dedup, documented counts | 20% |
| training engineering: resumable, tracked, correct schedules, scaling experiment with a tested prediction | 25% |
| post-training done and measured, including what it broke | 15% |
| report and model card | 10% |

A result that says "my DPO run made the model worse, and here's why" scores higher than an unexplained improvement.
