# Part I: Foundations

Python, linear algebra, calculus, probability, statistics, information theory and computer systems. This is the part people want to skip. It is also the part that separates engineers who understand why their model broke from engineers who restart the kernel and pray.

About 255 hours in total.

| Module | Hours | What it's about |
|---|---|---|
| [01. Python for Engineers](01-python-for-engineers/) | 40 | Not "Python for beginners". Python for people who will write code that other people run at 3 AM. |
| [02. Linear Algebra](02-linear-algebra/) | 45 | Every model in this course is, at the bottom, a pile of matrix multiplications. Let's understand them. |
| [03. Calculus and Optimization](03-calculus-optimization/) | 40 | Training a model is minimizing a function. This module is about how you minimize functions, and why it sometimes doesn't work. |
| [04. Probability](04-probability/) | 40 | Machine learning is applied probability wearing a hoodie. You need to speak it fluently. |
| [05. Statistics and Experimentation](05-statistics-experimentation/) | 40 | The part of data science that decides whether your "improvement" is real or just noise with a nice chart. |
| [06. Information Theory](06-information-theory/) | 15 | Short module, huge payoff. Cross-entropy loss, KL divergence, perplexity and half of the generative modeling literature suddenly make sense. |
| [07. Computer Systems for ML](07-computer-systems/) | 35 | Your model runs on a real machine with real memory, real floating point and a real network. Ignore that and it will remind you, usually in production. |
